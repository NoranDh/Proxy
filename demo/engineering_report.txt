ENGINEERING DEPARTMENT - PROCESS AND DESIGN DATA (SAMPLE DATA FOR DEMONSTRATION)

Equipment: Crude charge pump P-101A, crude distillation unit
Service: stabilized crude oil, pumping temperature 145 C
Material: carbon steel casing, 12% Cr impeller, AISI 4140 shaft

Design and operating conditions:
- Rated flow 420 m3/h at 185 m head; best efficiency point 430 m3/h
- Suction pressure 3.2 barg, discharge pressure 18.5 barg
- Operating point at the time of failure: 410 m3/h (95% of best efficiency point), steady
- No process upset, no flow or pressure excursions in the 7 days before failure
- NPSH available 9.5 m against 5.2 m required
- Ambient temperature 38-44 C during the week of the failure

Sequence of events:
- 11 March 09:15  Vibration notification raised by Inspection (priority 3)
- 13 March 02:30  Bearing temperature high alarm (85 C) acknowledged by the panel operator; no field check recorded
- 14 March 00:40  Bearing temperature reached 96 C
- 14 March 02:40  Pump tripped on bearing temperature high-high (110 C) and seized
- 14 March 02:55  Standby pump P-101B started; unit throughput reduced by 15% for 4 hours

Engineering review:
- Manufacturer specifies an oil change every 3 months or 2000 running hours for this bearing housing at pumping temperatures above 120 C. The pump had run about 6500 hours since the last oil change.
- The preventive maintenance task interval in the maintenance system is set to 3 months, but the system allows unlimited deferral without engineering approval.
- The high-temperature alarm response procedure requires a field check within one shift. It was not carried out.
- Pump selection, hydraulic loading and alignment are within design limits. There is no indication of a design deficiency.
- The sister pump P-101B has the same lubrication arrangement and the same deferred oil-change tasks.
